First patch-synthesis prototype: numbers are close, seams are not

tools/patch_synth.py builds a patch database from 15 of the reference's 16 regions, holds out r.3.3,
and synthesises a 512x512 terrain there. Quilting is on a fixed lattice with hash-chosen patches, so
it is deterministic and needs no search at runtime.

Two findings on the way:
1. Blending raw patches produces a false gradient of (h_i - h_j)/cell wherever four patches meet:
   slope p50 came out 1.73 against the reference's 0.71. Splitting each patch into a large-scale level
   and a high-frequency detail fixed it (0.93).
2. Patch synthesis alone cannot invent relief. With the level field taken from the patches the
   synthesised terrain had relief 101 blocks against the reference's 261; the map's relief lives in the
   arrangement of its landforms, not in their local texture.

So the prototype is a hybrid: the analytic generator supplies the large-scale layout, the patches
supply the local texture. Held-out comparison against the real r.3.3:

    metric       real r.3.3   hybrid
    relief           261        257
    slope p50       0.71       0.93
    slope p90       1.50       1.74
    ridge           1.18       0.25
    hollow          8.03       6.78
    footslope       1.62       2.29
    valley          0.32       0.03

Hollow and footslope, the two classes the analytic model could not reach at all, are now close.

The picture shows the remaining problem: the 48-block lattice is visible as straight seams. Grid
quilting without the overlap search that makes Efros-Freeman seamless produces exactly this. The next
step is either a proper greedy quilting pass computed offline into one large tileable field (the mod
then samples that field with a per-region offset, so no runtime search and no seams), or
gradient-domain blending over a wider feather.
