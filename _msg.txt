Fit the snow separately from the terrain (and it turns out to be pure altitude)

tools/fit_snow.py fits  logit P(snow) = (height - line) / width + climate(x,z)  against the observed
snow mask, and reports accuracy against trivial baselines.

Result on r.0.0: a height-only logistic already reaches logloss 0.391 and 81.5% accuracy
(always-no-snow is 63.9%). Adding nine climate blobs improved it *zero* times, so the snow pattern
carries no extra spatial structure -- it is altitude, with a 25-block transition at y=140, and the
remaining error is the inherent scatter of a binary field.

So the treatment in the mod is now: score = (height - snowLine)/snowWidth + snowNoise*mask, snow
where score > 0. With line 140 / width 25 the generated world covers 63.1% against the reference's
63.9%.

What does not match yet is the shape of the curve: the generated terrain has almost no columns
between y=80 and 120 (the reference has 21.8% of its area there with snow), so our transition looks
sharper. That is a terrain-distribution problem, not a snow-rule problem -- the skeleton fit lacks the
reference's low basin.
