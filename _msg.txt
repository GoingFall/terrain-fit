Tiled residual: the tiling is clean, the layout is the bottleneck

tools/tile_synth.py implements the infinite-friendly scheme: a layout function plus a pool of
high-frequency residual tiles selected by hash and combined by normalised overlap-add (Hann windows at
50% hop, so the weights sum to exactly 1.000 everywhere -- verified).

Three defects were found and fixed while chasing visible straight lines:
1. each tile's residual had a non-zero mean, which is a constant offset per tile and shows as a faint
   rectangular step; the windowed mean is now removed per tile.
2. contrast normalisation over a small window clipped at its limit and produced its own bright lines;
   the clip is now conservative.
3. neither of those was the real cause. The lines sit at columns 63/223/383, spaced 160 = the analytic
   layout's cellSize, and the layout alone reproduces them with a max/median gradient ratio of 9.84.
   They are the cone creases of the analytic generator, the same defect identified several rounds ago
   and never fixed.

With a crease-free fBm layout the spikes collapse to a ratio of 1.41, i.e. within noise. So the tiling
machinery works; what it cannot do is invent structure:

    layout        relief  slope50  ridge  hollow  footslope
    reference       261    0.71    1.18    8.03     1.62
    fBm + tiles     197    1.05    0.02   10.22     4.21

The fBm layout has no ridges and no valleys, so the residual dominates and the result reads as noise.
The analytic layout has structure but carries creases.

Conclusion: the missing piece is a layout generator that is structured and crease-free. That is a much
smaller problem than the whole terrain -- it only needs a 128x128 or 256x256 field, where a small
generative model is cheap to train and to sample -- and the tiled residual then supplies the texture.
