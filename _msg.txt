Mechanism tests: the analytic landform model has a ceiling, and it is below the reference

Three mechanisms were added to the Python mirror and swept against the reference's landform
distribution (ridge 0.39-6.06, hollow 2.99-11.78, footslope 0.50-2.84 across 16 regions):

    mechanism                  ridge        hollow       footslope
    smooth union (k up to .25)  0.93 -> 0.35  4.75 -> 6.47  0.77 -> 0.63
    toe collar (depth .35)      0.14          2.49 -> 4.33  0.16 -> 0.74
    valley troughs (depth 40)   0.00          2.52 -> 4.11  0.13 -> 0.04

Every mechanism moves one class at the cost of another, because ridge, hollow and footslope are all
driven by the same few geometric parameters (profile exponent, radius, composition). Forcing the
footslope up with a toe collar collapses the ridge class; carving valleys raises hollow and removes
footslope entirely. The reachable envelope stays around hollow <= 5.5 and footslope <= 0.75, against a
reference that reaches 11.78 and 2.84.

That is the answer to "what model can this map support": not a parameterised landform model. The map's
landform classes are jointly distributed, and a five-parameter geometric generator cannot move in that
joint space -- it only moves along one axis at a time.

A patch-based model has no such coupling: it reproduces the joint distribution by construction because
the patches are samples of it. That is the model the map's ~4 million heights actually support, and the
acceptance test is ready for it: synthesise a region held out of the database and compare landform
distribution, slope percentiles and relief against the real region.
