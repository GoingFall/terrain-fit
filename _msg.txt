Adopt geomorphons (the descriptor behind PTRM) as the structural metric

Five marginal statistics failed to separate our terrain from the reference (tile variance, curvature,
level-set curvature, facet coherence, slope percentiles -- all matched while the pictures clearly
differ). Searching the literature turned up PTRM (Rajasekaran et al., ACM TAP 2022), a *perceptual*
terrain realism metric built on geomorphons: every cell is classified into one of ten landforms
(flat, peak, ridge, shoulder, spur, slope, hollow, valley, footslope, pit) from an eight-direction
ternary pattern, and the distribution of those classes is what correlates with human realism
judgements. Their finding is that synthetic terrain looks synthetic precisely because it lacks
valleys, ridges, hollows and spurs.

tools/geomorphons.py implements the classifier and prints the distribution.

At radius 30 blocks, flat tolerance 2:

    landform     reference   generated
    ridge           2.40%       1.29%
    hollow          4.53%       3.42%
    valley          0.26%       1.82%
    footslope       1.16%       0.64%
    spur            4.61%       4.42%
    slope          85.55%      86.85%

This is the first descriptor that separates the two, and it is interpretable: our terrain is a field of
convex domes whose gaps read as *valleys* (7x too many), while the reference is *ridges* with concave
*hollows* along their flanks. That is a concrete model change: landforms should be ridge lines with
curvature variation along the slope, not domes.
