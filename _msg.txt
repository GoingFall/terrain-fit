Held-out test: a spatial style partition cannot be justified

Question: the generator must decide, at a position it was never fitted on, what the terrain should
look like there. The reference map has 16 regions with strongly varying statistics (ridge 0.39-6.06,
hollow 2.99-11.78). Is that variation predictable from position -- and therefore worth modelling with
provinces or a continuous style field?

tools/holdout_check.py fits three position -> statistics structures on half the regions and predicts
the other half (60 random 8/8 splits). Error is normalised by the map's own std, so 1.0 means "no
better than always predicting the mean":

    structure    MAE    median   p90    vs global
    global      0.836   0.838   0.955   baseline
    provinces   0.888   0.898   1.038   -6%
    field       0.818   0.806   0.936   +2%

Spatially separated split (train the top two rows, test the bottom two):

    global 0.928   provinces 1.083   field 0.984

Conclusions:
1. The regional variation of this map is essentially unpredictable from position: the best structure
   beats "always predict the mean" by 2%, and it loses that advantage on a separated split.
2. The discrete-province design -- the one that looked most promising -- is the *worst*, both for
   transferability and for prediction. Dropped.
3. So the variation is not a spatial pattern to be reproduced; it is variation to be matched in
   distribution. The generator should draw its regional parameter set from the measured distribution
   (ridge 0.4-6, hollow 3-12, relief 151-340) instead of trying to place provinces on the map.

That is also the more transferable design: a new map needs its 16 regional measurements, not a fit of
where its provinces are.
