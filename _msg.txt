Training cost measured on the local GPU; the bottleneck turned out not to be time

A CUDA build of torch was already installed in five conda environments (all see the RTX 5070 as
sm_120 / CUDA 12.8), so nothing had to be installed. Using medical_report (torch 2.10.0+cu128):

    training      CPU 4.8 steps/s   GPU 70 steps/s   (14.6x)
    12k steps     --                222 s
    33.5k steps   --                480 s   (the run is capped by --minutes, default 8)
    100k steps    --                ~25 min
    500k steps    --                ~2 h

Sampling is *slower* on the GPU (2.0 s per 64x64 tile against 0.67 s on CPU): the tile is too small
for 200 sequential kernel launches to amortise.

Quality at 12k and at 33.5k steps is the same - smooth blobs, with a per-tile std of 0.09-0.15 against
0.198 in the training data. The model is averaging toward the mean, which is what an undertrained
diffusion model looks like.

But the more useful finding is about the data rather than the model: at 4x downsampling the *training
crops themselves* are smooth blobs with only a few terraced edges (see preview/images/layout_real_crops.png).
So a model that reproduces them faithfully would produce blobs too. 4x downsampling is too coarse to be
where the interesting structure lives; the ridges and valleys are at 1x-2x.

Conclusion: the cost of training is not the obstacle (minutes, not days). The open question is whether a
learned layout is needed at all at this scale, or whether a non-parametric low-frequency sampler would
do the same job for zero training.
